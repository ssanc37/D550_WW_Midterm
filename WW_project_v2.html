Introduction

Wastewater based epidemiology(WBE) has reemerged as a public health surveillance tool for tracking infectious diseases at the community level through the analysis of sewage. Increased interest occurred during the COVID-19 (SARS-COV-2) pandemic, with the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) establishing the National Wastewater Surveillance System (NWSS) partnering with major projects like WastewaterSCAN.

This report utilizes the CDC Wastewater Viral Activity Level (WVAL), which records weekly surveillance measurements across U.S wastewater treatment faculties for respiratory pathogens SARS-COV-2, Influenza A and Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV).

Objectives

  1. Load the wastewater surveillance dataset from the online repository (Limited to 50,000 rows of data, since the dataset has over 500K)

  2. Construct summary tables evaluating categorical viral activity

  3. Visualize the viral activity levels over time to identify seasonal trends

Load data

Data loaded from data.cdc.gov via url.

Variables of interest:

pathogen_target: Pathogen for which wastewater viral activity levels calculated

week_end: Last date in one week time period for CDC/MMWR reporting week

Site_WVAL_Category: Category for the calculated site WVAL for the CDC/MMWR reporting week. Categories are ordinal based on pathogen specific cut-offs of WVAL values: very low, low, moderate, high, very high; limited/no data: category when there is insufficient data to calculate a WVAL.

Site_WVAL: The calculated site wastewater viral activity level (WVAL) for the CDC/MMWR reporting week

Visualizing Analysis

To observing pathogen trajectories over 2026, I aggregated the mean weekly site viral activity metric (site_WVAL).

The line graph above illustrates the evolution of average viral concentration levels in U.S. wastewater from June 2026 to September 2026. The x-axis tracks weekly reporting dates (week_end), while the y-axis reflects the average calculated viral activity level (mean_wval). Pathogens are color coded, Influenza A in red, RSV in blue, SARS-COV-2 in green. SARS-COV-2 has maintained activity throughout the year, with a peak in August with a mean viral activity of 700. Influenza A started to show a minor peak of viral activity around 75. RSV has remained near zero throughout this time period, with a minor peak of 50 viral activity around July.
